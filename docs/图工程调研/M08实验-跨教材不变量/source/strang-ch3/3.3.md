## 3.3. The Complete Solution to Ax = b

Every matrix A with full column rank ( r = n) has all these properties:

1. All columns of A are pivot columns.

2. There are no free variables or special solutions.

3. The nullspace N(A) contains only the zero vector x = 0.

4. If Ax = b has a solution (it might not) then it has only one solution.

In the essential language of the next section, this A has independent columns. $A x = \mathbf { 0 }$ only happens when $x = 0$ . In Chapter 4 we will add one more fact to the list: The square matrix $\bar { A } ^ { \mathrm { T } }$ A is invertible when the rank is n.

In this case the nullspace of A (and R) has shrunk to the zero vector. The solution to Ax = b is unique (if it exists). There will be $m - n$ zero rows in R. So there are m - n conditions on b in order to have $0 = 0$ in those rows, and b in the column space. With full column rank, Ax = b has one solution or no solution $( m > n$ is overdetermined).

## 3.3. The Complete Solution to Ax = b

We are ready for the definition of linear independence, as soon as we summarize the four possibilities-which depend on the rank. Notice how r, m, n are the critical numbers.

The four possibilities for linear equations depend on the rank r

$$
\begin{array}{l l l l l l} \boldsymbol {r} = \boldsymbol {m} & \text {and} & \boldsymbol {r} = \boldsymbol {n} & \text {Square and invertible} & A \boldsymbol {x} = \boldsymbol {b} & \text {has 1 solution} \\ \boldsymbol {r} = \boldsymbol {m} & \text {and} & r <   n & \text {Short and wide} & A \boldsymbol {x} = \boldsymbol {b} & \text {has \infty solutions} \\ r <   m & \text {and} & \boldsymbol {r} = \boldsymbol {n} & \text {Tall and thin} & A \boldsymbol {x} = \boldsymbol {b} & \text {has 0 or 1 solution} \\ r <   m & \text {and} & r <   n & \text {Not full rank} & A \boldsymbol {x} = \boldsymbol {b} & \text {has 0 or \infty solutions} \end{array}
$$

The reduced R will fall in the same category as the matrix A. In case the pivot columns happen to come first, we can display these four possibilities for R. For $R x = d$ (and the original $A x = b )$ to be solvable, d must end in $m - r$ zeros. Fis the free part of R.

$$
\begin{array}{l l l l l} \text {Four types for R} & [ I ] & [ I F ] & [ I ] & [ I F ] \\ \text {Their ranks} & r = m = n & r = m <   n & r = n <   m & r <   m, r <   n \end{array}
$$

Cases 1 and 2 have full row rank $r = m$ . Cases 1 and 3 have full column rank $r = n$ Case 4 is the most general in theory and it is the least common in practice.

## • REVIEW OF THE KEY IDEAS •

1. The rank r is the number of pivots. The matrix R has m - r zero rows.

2. Ax = bis solvable if and only if the last m - r equations reduce to $0 = 0$

3. One particular solution $\scriptstyle x _ { p }$ has all free variables equal to zero.

4. The pivot variables are determined after the free variables are chosen.

S. Full column rank r = n means no free variables: one solution or none.

6. Full row rank r = m means one solution if $m = n$ or infinitely many if $m < n$
