 Abstract 
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} is a version of Church’s type theory with global quotation
and evaluation operators that is engineered to reason about the
interplay of syntax and semantics and to formalize syntax-based
mathematical algorithms. ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} is a variant of ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} 
that admits undefined expressions, partial functions, and multiple
base types of individuals. It is better suited than ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} as a
logic for building networks of theories connected by theory morphisms.
This paper presents the syntax and semantics of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} , defines
a notion of a theory morphism from one ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} theory to another,
and gives two simple examples involving monoids that illustrate the
use of theory morphisms in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} .
 1 Introduction
 A syntax-based mathematical algorithm (SBMA) , such as a
symbolic differentiation algorithm, manipulates mathematical
expressions in a mathematically meaningful way. Reasoning about SBMAs
requires reasoning about the relationship between how the expressions
are manipulated and what the manipulations mean mathematically. We
argue in  [ 8 ] that a logic with quotation and evaluation would
provide a global infrastructure for formalizing SBMAs and reasoning
about the interplay of syntax and semantics that is embodied in them.
 Quotation is a mechanism for referring to a syntactic value
(e.g., a syntax tree) that represents the syntactic structure of an
expression, while evaluation is a mechanism for referring to
the value of the expression that a syntactic value represents.
Incorporating quotation and evaluation into a traditional logic like
first-order logic or simple type theory is tricky; there are several
challenging problems that the logic engineer must
overcome  [ 8 , 9 ] .
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe}   [ 9 , 10 ] is a version of Church’s
type theory with global quotation and evaluation operators inspired by
the quote and eval operators in the Lisp programming language. We
show in  [ 9 ] that formula schemas and meaning formulas
for SBMAs can be expressed in ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} using quotation and
evaluation and that such schemas and meaning formulas can be
instantiated and proved within the proof system for ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} .
 The little theories method   [ 11 ] is an approach
for understanding and organizing mathematical knowledge as a
 theory graph   [ 14 ] consisting of axiomatic
 theories as nodes and theory morphisms 1 1 
 1 
 Theory
morphisms are also known as immersions , realizations ,
 theory interpretations , translations , and
 views . as directed edges. A theory consists of a
 language of expressions that denote mathematical values and a
set of axioms that express in the language assumptions about
the values. A theory morphism is a meaning-preserving mapping from
the formulas of one theory to the formulas of another theory. Theory
morphisms serve as information conduits that enable definitions and
theorems to be passed from an abstract theory to many other more
concrete theories  [ 2 ] .
 A biform theory   [ 3 , 6 ] is a
combination of an axiomatic theory and an algorithmic theory (a
collection of algorithms that perform symbolic computations). It
consists of a language L L generated from a set of
 symbols , a set of transformers , and a set of
 axioms . The expressions of L L denote mathematical values that
include syntactic values representing the expressions of L L . The
transformers are SBMAs and other algorithms that implement functions
on the expressions of L L and are represented by symbols of L L . The
axioms are formulas of L L that express properties about the symbols
and transformers of the biform theory. Unlike traditional logics,
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} is well suited for formalizing biform theories. Can the
little theories method be applied to biform theories formalized in
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} ? This would require a definition of a theory morphism for
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} theories.
 Defining a notion of a theory morphism in a logic with quotation is
not as straightforward as in a logic without quotation due to the
following problem:
 Constant Interpretation Problem . Let T 1 T_{1} and T 2 T_{2} 
be theories in a logic with a quotation operator
 ⌜ ⋅ ⌝ \ulcorner\cdot\urcorner . If a theory morphism Φ \Phi from T 1 T_{1} to
 T 2 T_{2} interprets two distinct constants c c and c ′ c^{\prime} in T 1 T_{1} by a
single constant d d in T 2 T_{2} , then Φ \Phi would map the true
formula ⌜ ​ c ​ ⌝ ≠ ⌜ ​ c ′ ​ ⌝ \ulcorner c\urcorner\not=\ulcorner c^{\prime}\urcorner of T 1 T_{1} to the false
formula ⌜ ​ d ​ ⌝ ≠ ⌜ ​ d ​ ⌝ \ulcorner d\urcorner\not=\ulcorner d\urcorner of T 2 T_{2} , and hence
 Φ \Phi would not be meaning preserving. Similarly, if Φ \Phi 
interprets c c as an expression e e in T 2 T_{2} that is not a
constant, then Φ \Phi would map a true formula like
 is-constant ​ ( ⌜ ​ c ​ ⌝ ) \mbox{\sf is-constant}(\ulcorner c\urcorner) to the false formula
 is-constant ​ ( ⌜ ​ e ​ ⌝ ) \mbox{\sf is-constant}(\ulcorner e\urcorner) .
 This paper defines a notion of a theory morphism that overcomes this
problem in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} , a variant of ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} that admits
undefined expressions, partial functions, and multiple base types of
individuals. ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} merges the machinery for quotation and
evaluation found in ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe}   [ 9 ] with the
machinery for undefinedness found in 𝒬 0 u {\cal Q}^{\rm u}_{0}   [ 7 ] . Like
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} and 𝒬 0 u {\cal Q}^{\rm u}_{0} , ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} is based on
 𝒬 0 {\cal Q}_{0}   [ 1 ] , Peter Andrews’ elegant version of Church’s
type theory. See  [ 9 ] for references related to
 ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} .
 ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} is better suited than ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} as a logic for the
little theories method for two reasons. First, it is often convenient
for a theory morphism from T 1 T_{1} to T 2 T_{2} to interpret different kinds
of values by values of different types. Since ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} contains
only one base type of individuals, ι \iota , all individuals in a
theory T 1 T_{1} must be interpreted by values of the same type in T 2 T_{2} .
Allowing multiple base types of individuals in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} eliminates
this restriction. Second, it is often useful to interpret a type
 α \alpha in T 1 T_{1} by a subset of the denotation of a type β \beta in
 T 2 T_{2} . As shown in  [ 4 ] , this naturally leads to partial
functions on the type β \beta . ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} has built-in support for
partial functions and undefinedness based on the traditional approach
to undefinedness  [ 5 ] ; ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} has no such built-in
support. 2 2 
 2 
 A logic without support for partial functions and
undefinedness — such as ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} or the logic of
HOL  [ 12 ] — can interpret α \alpha by a type
 β ′ \beta^{\prime} that is isomorphic to a subset of β \beta . However, this
approach is more complicated and farther from standard mathematics
practice than interpreting α \alpha directly by a subset of
 β \beta . 
 The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The syntax and
semantics of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} are presented in sections  2 
and  3 . The notion of a theory morphism in
 ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} is defined in section  4 .
Section  5 contains two simple examples of theory
morphisms in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} involving monoids. The paper concludes in
section  6 with a summary of the paper’s results and
some brief remarks about constructing theory morphisms in an
implementation of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} and about future work.
 The syntax and semantics of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} are presented as briefly as
possible. The reader should consult  [ 7 ] 
and  [ 9 ] for a more in-depth discussion on the ideas
underlying the syntax and semantics in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} . Due to limited
space, a proof system is not given in this paper for ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} . A
proof system for ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} can be straightforwardly derived by
merging the proof systems for ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe}   [ 9 ] and
 𝒬 0 u {\cal Q}^{\rm u}_{0}   [ 7 ] .
 2 Syntax
 The syntax of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} is the same as the syntax of
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe}   [ 9 ] except that (1) the types include
denumerably many base types of individuals instead of just the single
 ι \iota type, (2) the expressions include conditional expressions, and
(3) the logical constants include constants for definite description
and exclude is-expr ϵ → o \mbox{\sf is-expr}_{\epsilon\rightarrow o} — which we will
see is not needed since all constructions are “proper” in
 ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} .

 3 Semantics
 The semantics of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} is the same as the semantics of
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} except that the former admits undefined expressions in

valuation function for expressions is made into a partial function
that assigns a value to an expression iff the expression is defined
according to the traditional approach.

 4 Theory Morphisms
 In this section we define a “semantic morphism” of ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} that
maps the valid semantically closed formulas of one normal theory to
the valid semantically closed formulas of another normal theory.
Theory morphisms usually map base types to types. By exploiting the
support for partial functions in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} , we introduce a more
general notion of theory morphism that maps base types to semantically
closed predicates that represent sets of values of the same type.
This requires mapping expressions denoting functions on the base type
to expressions denoting functions with domains restricted to the
semantically closed predicate.

and so ℳ 2 ⊨ ν ¯ ​ ( A o ) \mbox{$\cal M$}_{2}\vDash\overline{\nu}(\textbf{A}_{o}) by ( ⋆ ⋆ ) (\star\star) in
the proof of Lemma  4.2.2 . □ \Box 
 Theorem 4.2.4 (Relative Satisfiability) 
 Let T 1 T_{1} and T 2 T_{2} be normal theories and suppose Φ \Phi is a
semantic morphism of from T 1 T_{1} to T 2 T_{2} . Then there is a general
model for T 1 T_{1} if there is a general model for T 2 T_{2} . 
 Proof 
 Let Φ = ( μ , ν ) \Phi=(\mu,\nu) be a semantic morphism from T 1 T_{1} to T 2 T_{2} ,
 ℳ 2 \mbox{$\cal M$}_{2} be a general model for T 1 T_{1} , and ℳ 1 \mbox{$\cal M$}_{1} be extracted from
 ℳ 2 \mbox{$\cal M$}_{2} as above. Since Φ \Phi is a semantic morphism, each of its
obligations is valid in T 2 T_{2} . Hence, ℳ 1 \mbox{$\cal M$}_{1} is a general model for
 T 1 T_{1} by Lemma  4.2.2 . □ \Box

 6 Conclusion
 ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} is a version of Church’s type theory with quotation and
evaluation described in great detail in  [ 9 ] . In this
paper we have (1) presented ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} , a variant of ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} 
that admits undefined expressions, partial functions, and multiple
base types of individuals, (2) defined a notion of a theory morphism
in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} , and (3) given two simple examples that illustrate the
use of theory morphisms in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} . The theory morphisms of
 ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} overcome the Constant Interpretation Problem discussed in
section  1 by requiring constants to be
injectively mapped to constants. Since ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} admits partial
functions, ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} theory morphisms are able to map base types to
sets of values of the same type — which enables many additional
natural meaning-preserving mappings between theories to be directly
defined as ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} theory morphisms. Thus the paper demonstrates
how theory morphisms can be defined in a traditional logic with
quotation and evaluation and how support for partial functions in a
traditional logic can be leveraged to obtain a wider class of theory
morphisms.
 The two examples presented in section  5 show that
constructing a translation in ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} from a theory T 1 T_{1} to a
theory T 2 T_{2} will often require defining new dummy constants in T 2 T_{2} .
This is certainly a significant inconvenience. However, it is an
inconvenience that can be greatly ameliorated in an implementation of
 ctt uqe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm uqe} by allowing a user to define a “pre-translation” that
is automatically transformed into a bona fide translation. A
pre-translation from T 1 T_{1} and T 2 T_{2} would be a pair ( μ , ν ) (\mu,\nu) where
 μ \mu maps base types to either types or semantically closed
predicates, ν \nu maps constants to expressions that need not be
constants, and ν \nu is not required to be injective. From the
pre-translation, the system would automatically extend T 2 T_{2} to a
theory T 2 ′ T^{\prime}_{2} and then construct a translation from T 1 T_{1} to T 2 ′ T^{\prime}_{2} .
 Our long-range goal is to implement a system for developing biform
theory graphs utilizing logics equipped with quotation and evaluation.
The next step in this direction is to implement ctt qe \mbox{\sc ctt}_{\rm qe} by
extending HOL Light  [ 13 ] , a simple implementation of
HOL  [ 12 ] .
