## 核心内容

- Hermann Ebbinghaus (1880s): forgetting curve = "a graph portraying the loss of learned information over time"; "postulated that it can be curbed by reviewing such information at several intervals over a period of time."
- C. A. Mace (1932): "Acts of revision should be spaced in gradually increasing intervals, roughly intervals of one day, two days, four days, eight days, and so on."
- Landauer & Bjork (1978): expanding rehearsal schedule "yield a greater percentage of accuracy at test points" (face-name associations).
- Pashler, Rohrer, Cepeda, & Carpenter (2007): spaced repetition "participants given the spaced repetition learning tasks showed higher scores on a final test" even for mathematical principle learning.
- Testing effect combined with spaced repetition improves long-term memory. "spacing increases the effort for retrieval, and that expanding intervals allow to gradually increase that difficulty" -> deeper processing.
- Bui et al. (2013): "participants with a higher working memory benefited from spaced repetition."
- Karpicke & Roediger (2007): "Expanding repetition practice promotes short-term retention, but equally spaced repetition enhances long-term retention."
- Leitner system (1973): cards grouped by mastery level; success -> next group; failure -> back to first group; each group longer intervals.
- SM family (SuperMemo): SM-0 to SM-18.
- FSRS: based on SSP-MMC framework ("Stochastic Shortest Path Minimize Memorization Cost"); "available in Anki starting with release 23.10 and in RemNote starting with release 1.16"; predictive modeling for "the most effective timing for review sessions."
- Meta-analysis Latimier, Peyre, & Ramus (2021): "'strong recommendations to teachers and students in favor of spaced retrieval practice are warranted.'"
- Tabibian et al. (2019): "the best schedule is learner-dependent, making general recommendations irrelevant."
- Robertson et al. (2023): spaced repetition into six-week simulation module improved residents' surgical proficiency.
