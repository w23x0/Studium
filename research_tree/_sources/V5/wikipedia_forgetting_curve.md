## 核心内容

Hermann Ebbinghaus (German psychologist, research 1880-1885) "studied the memorisation of nonsense syllables, such as 'WID' and 'ZOF'" by testing himself after various intervals.

- The forgetting curve "measures the decline of memory retention across time, showing how information is lost when there is no attempt to retain it."
- "purports to show that humans tend to halve their memory of newly learned knowledge in a matter of days or weeks unless they consciously review the learned material."
- Ebbinghaus published an equation: b = 100k/(log(t))^c + k, where "b represents 'Savings' expressed as a percentage, and t represents time in minutes." Constants c and k are 1.25 and 1.84 respectively.
- "A savings of 100% would indicate that all items were still known from the first trial" (75% savings = relearning required only 25% of original time).
- A 2015 replication "with one study subject has shown the experimental results similar to Ebbinghaus's original data."
- Ebbinghaus hypothesized "each repetition in learning increases the optimum interval before the next repetition is needed." He found "the forgetting curve was flattened by every repetition."
- Best methods for strengthening memory: "better memory representation (e.g. with mnemonic techniques)" and "repetition based on active recall (especially spaced repetition)."
- "waiting 10–20% of the time towards when the information will be needed is the optimum time for a single review."
- Principles underpin modern tools: Anki, SuperMemo, Quizlet.
