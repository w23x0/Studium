SNAPSHOT: Wikipedia — Adaptive learning URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_learning Fetched: 2026-08-13 via WebFetch --- [Extracted content] ## Content selection based on a learner model Adaptive systems use a "student model" to track learner skill levels. The simplest approach mirrors computerized adaptive testing (CAT), where "the subject is presented with questions that are selected based on their level of difficulty in relation to the presumed skill level of the subject." Algorithm like binary search: "always giving the subject a question which is halfway between what the computer has already determined to be the subject's maximum and minimum possible skill levels." Beyond correctness, systems analyze why students err: "a student who answers (b) is adding the exponents and failing to grasp the concept of like terms." ## Learning paths / instructional model The instructional model maps lessons to assessed skill levels. "When the student's level has been satisfactorily determined, the instructional model provides the appropriate lesson." More advanced models "organize its lessons by concept" and can "analyze the collection of weaknesses and tailor a lesson plan accordingly." ## Tool sophistication spectrum Entry-level: basic branching — "A correct answer may take the learner to Path A, whereas an incorrect answer may take them to Path B." Advanced tools "utilize AI methods such as an inference engine" and provide "rich assessment capabilities." ## Efficacy Among 37 studies, "an overwhelming majority of 86% (32 studies) reported positive effects."
